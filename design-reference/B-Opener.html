The Slow Return of the Hedgerow

After decades of removal, farmers are replanting the tangled field boundaries that once stitched the countryside together.

[Author Name]
14 min read

In late autumn, when the leaves have gone and the structure of a field boundary is visible again, you can read a hedge like a ledger. Hawthorn for the stock-proofing, hazel for poles, a single oak left standing because someone, two centuries ago, decided it should be.

For most of the last century the ledger was being torn out. Larger machines wanted larger fields, and the boundaries that slowed them were grubbed up by the mile.

The reversal has been quiet. It happens in winter, with spades and spiral guards, on the margins of farms that would never describe themselves as conservation projects.
