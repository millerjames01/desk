The Slow Return of the Hedgerow 03 — Nature

Planting is the easy part. A young hedge needs years of cutting back before it thickens at the base, and the temptation is always to let it run upward into a line of thin trees that keeps nothing in and shelters nothing below.

The art of laying

The old answer is laying: cutting each stem most of the way through near the ground, bending it over, and weaving it between stakes. The partly severed stems keep growing, now sideways, and the hedge becomes dense from the soil up.

[Black-and-white photograph]
A laid hedge in its first spring, pleachers still visible.

Done well, it lasts for decades. Done badly, it is obvious within a season, which may be why the craft has survived in competitions long after it vanished from ordinary farm work.
