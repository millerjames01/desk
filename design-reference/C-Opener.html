I

The Slow Return
of the Hedgerow

[Author Name]

In late autumn, when the leaves have gone and the structure of a field boundary is visible again, you can read a hedge like a ledger. Hawthorn for the stock-proofing, hazel for poles, a single oak left standing because someone, two centuries ago, decided it should be.

For most of the last century the ledger was being torn out. Larger machines wanted larger fields, and the boundaries that slowed them were grubbed up by the mile. What was lost was not only shelter for birds but a way of dividing land that kept water, soil and wildlife moving at walking pace.

The reversal has been quiet. It happens in winter, with spades and spiral guards.
